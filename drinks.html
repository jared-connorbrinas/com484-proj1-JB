Coffee Basics: Find Your First Order

These ratios are useful starting points. Café recipes and serving sizes vary, especially for milk drinks.

Espresso Shot

A small, concentrated coffee brewed under pressure. Try it when you want to taste the coffee without milk.

Brew ratio: 1:2, dry ground coffee to liquid espresso by weight.

Example double shot: 18 g grounds → 36 g espresso. This is the output in your cup, not the water added to the machine. A single shot uses a smaller dose; ask how many shots the café serves.

Cortado

A small espresso drink softened by warm, lightly textured milk. A good option when you want coffee flavor with a little creaminess.

Drink ratio: approximately 1:1, espresso to milk by volume.

Example: 30 mL espresso + about 30 mL milk. Actual café sizes vary.

Cappuccino

Espresso with steamed milk and an airy foam layer. Choose this if you enjoy a fluffy texture.

Classic guide: approximately 1:1:1, espresso to steamed milk to foam by volume.

Example: 30 mL espresso + 30 mL steamed milk + about 30 mL foam. Equal thirds are a simplified guide, not a rule for every café; foam contains air and is not a separate milk ingredient.

Flat White

Espresso with silky milk and a thin layer of fine foam, called microfoam. Choose it for a creamy texture with noticeable coffee flavor.

Example drink ratio: approximately 1:2, espresso to milk by volume.

Example: 30 mL espresso + about 60 mL milk. Shot count and cup size differ between cafés.

Pour Over

Hot water poured over ground coffee in a filter. This makes a longer black coffee that can highlight the beans’ individual flavors.

Starter brew ratio: 1:16, dry ground coffee to total brewing water by weight.

Example: 20 g grounds + 320 g water. The finished cup weighs less because the grounds retain water. Adjust the recipe to your beans and taste.

How to Read a Coffee Ratio

1:2 means one part of the first ingredient for two parts of the second. Always check which ingredients and units the ratio describes.

  • Espresso brewing: grams of dry grounds compared with grams of espresso produced.
  • Pour over brewing: grams of dry grounds compared with grams of water poured.
  • Milk drinks: approximate volumes of brewed espresso and milk; cappuccino also describes foam volume.

More milk usually softens the taste of espresso. It does not remove caffeine from the shots already in the drink.

Americano

Quick Recommendations

Where should I start if I am new to coffee?

For a creamy drink, try a flat white or latte. For fluffy foam, try a cappuccino. If you want black coffee, try a pour over; for a small, concentrated drink with some milk, try a cortado.

What should I order if I like sweet drinks?

Try a caramel latte, vanilla latte, or strawberry matcha.

What should I order if I want less sugar?

Try cold brew, an Americano, or a plain matcha latte with less sweetener.

A note on matcha

The Japanese word for matcha is 抹茶(matcha).
